This thesis studies robust strategy optimization in systematic trading. The main question is how
trading strategies can be optimized on past market data while limiting optimization-induced
overfitting, defined as the risk that selected parameters fit noise or path-specific patterns in the
in-sample period rather than a stable market effect.

The project develops a C++ and Python research framework for backtesting, parameter optimization,
trade reconstruction, synthetic market generation, and report generation. The C++ component handles
simulation and optimization, while Python is used for plotting and performance analysis.

The empirical analysis compares two optimization workflows. The benchmark uses the standard IS/OOS
approach: parameters are selected on the historical in-sample period, defined as the first 70\% of the
original series, and then tested on the final 30\% OOS period. The alternative workflow uses the same
OOS test, but changes the optimization input. Instead of optimizing on one historical IS path, it
generates multiple synthetic market paths from the IS period and selects parameters using average
performance across those paths.

The comparison focuses on the deterioration from optimization performance to OOS performance. A
smaller IS/OOS performance gap is interpreted as evidence of lower overfitting. In the experiments,
the synthetic optimization method does not show a clear reduction in overfitting compared with the
standard IS/OOS benchmark. The synthetic paths are useful as alternative market scenarios, but they do
not lead to parameters that generalize better in the tested setting.
